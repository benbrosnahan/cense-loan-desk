# SCOPE-06: Repayment clock assumed to start this month

**P0** · effort M · Borrowers the model does not fit yet

Every repayment term restarts this month, so a borrower four years into Standard is shown $372 a month instead of their $555 bill and a debt-free date of November 2036 instead of November 2032, and the date slides later on every return visit.

| | |
|---|---|

## Measured

Four years into Standard (entered repayment owing $50,000 at 6.00%, 48 payments of $555.10 made, $33,494.75 owed, $555.10 typed into 'Minimum monthly payment'; income $45,000, 'Pay the least overall'): Plans hero 'Standard $372 a month'; Standard card 'Per month $372', 'In total, over 10 yr $44,623'; Extended 'Per month $216', 'In total, over 25 yr $64,742'; Graduated $167 to start over 10 yr; summary line '$372 a month on Standard'; Payoff 'ON STANDARD · $372 A MONTH REQUIRED', 'Debt free in November 2036', interest $11,128. '555' appears on none of the four tabs, and estimatePlans returns identical output with minPayment 0 or 555.10. Sweep 1 to 9 years in: the app's Standard falls from $513.32 (7.5% under the bill) to $71.61 (87.1% under) while Payoff says November 2036 every time; the true last payment moves from November 2035 to November 2027. The $100-extra headline claims 32 months and $3,149 of interest saved; at the real bill it is 13 months and $1,189. Fuzz of 20,000 synthetic borrowers 1 to 9 years into Standard: fixing only the clock changes the least-overall pick in 24.6% (3.7% at 1 year, 17.6% at 4, 54.0% at 9; 24.9% fixing Standard alone), 14.3% with a wider balance and income mix and 8.4% with incomes of $0 to $60,000. Every change is RAP or IBR to Standard; following the app's pick in those cases costs a median $1,199.91 more (p90 $5,417.82), and the app's Standard figure sits a median 43.0% below the real bill.
Return visit ($30,000 at 6.53% on Standard, $341.10 a month, record saved 2026-11-02, nothing retyped): 'Debt free in November 2036' becomes December 2036 at +30 days, May 2037 at +182 and November 2037 at +365, with the table still starting 'Today $30,000' and then 'Nov 2028 $27,801', although the first visit predicted $27,801 for Nov 2027. No tab says how old the balances are (only Records shows the record date), and 'Save today's snapshot' stores the year-old $30,000 as a 2027-11-03 record. Retyping the true $27,800.75 gives '$316 A MONTH REQUIRED' against the $341.10 bill and still November 2037.
In school (a sophomore's four 2025–26 and 2026–27 loans, $12,171.57, no income): hero 'Tiered Standard $138 a month', Payoff 'Debt free in December 2036' under 'What this assumes: payments start this month'; Plans says nothing about when payments start.

## Required

Standard keeps the payment set when the loan entered repayment: the $555.10 bill for the 72 payments left, $39,968 from today, $6,473 interest, last payment November 2032 (685.208(b)(1)(i)). Extended gets the 252 months left, not 300: $234.08 a month, $58,988 in total (685.208(b)(4)(i)); Graduated likewise has only the months left of its 10 years ((b)(6)(i)). Deferment and forbearance, including the March 2020 to September 2023 pause, do not count ((b)(1)(iv)), and switching plans keeps the time used (685.210(b)(2)(ii)(A), (b)(4)(i)(A)). After the retype the payment is still $341.10 with 108 payments left, ending November 2036. A stored balance carries the date it was entered and is not labelled 'Today' a year later. For the sophomore no payment is due before a first bill between about November 2029 and January 2030 (685.207), about $12,955 is owed then after unsubsidized interest, and the 10-year payment is about $146.03 to $146.85 rather than $137.96, ending 34 to 36 months after the app's date.

## Fix

Three parts. Part 1 takes under an hour; part 2 corrects the figures; part 3 dates the balances.

1. Say it (S). In plans.ts:278 change 'Two things these estimates assume.' to 'Three things these estimates assume.' and add: 'And they start every plan this month. If you are already repaying, Standard, Graduated and Extended keep the term that began when you entered repayment, and switching plans does not restart it, so your servicer's bill and end date are the real ones. If you are in school or your six-month grace period, nothing is due until grace ends, so payments start later and unsubsidized interest keeps building until then.' Put the same two cases after 'payments start this month' at RankingPanel.tsx:334. On the Standard card only (PlansPanel.tsx:152; never on IBR or RAP), when the typed bills add up to more than the Standard estimate plus Math.max(10, bill * 0.05), show a Notice: 'You entered $555 a month. If that is your Standard bill, it is the real figure: fewer than 10 years are left and the total is lower than shown.'

2. Use the borrower's own clock (M). Add one optional question to the Plans card (page.tsx:672), 'Are you repaying on the Standard plan now?', stored as Profile.onStandardNow (types/index.ts:110, default false). In buildContext (calc.ts:222), work out the months already used from the bill they typed, which already nets out deferment and forbearance, and trust it only when it can be a Standard bill (a real one is never below a fresh 10-year payment on today's balance):
```ts
const bill = loans.every((l) => l.minPayment > 0)
  ? loans.reduce((s, l) => s + l.minPayment, 0)
  : 0;
const r = rate / 1200;
let monthsUsed = 0;
if (profile.onStandardNow && bill >= amortizedPayment(balance, rate, 10) - 1 && bill > balance * r) {
  const x = r > 0 ? -Math.log(1 - (balance * r) / bill) / Math.log(1 + r) : balance / bill;
  monthsUsed = 120 - Math.min(120, Math.max(1, Math.ceil(x - 0.02))); // 48 here, so 72 left
}
```
Return monthsUsed on the context. Price Standard over left = 120 - ctx.monthsUsed months at calc.ts:340 (fixedTotals(balance, rate, left / 12) with years: left / 12), Graduated over the same months (give graduatedTotals at calc.ts:89 a months argument in place of the fixed 5 x 24 at :91-92), and Extended over 300 - ctx.monthsUsed at calc.ts:374. Payoff follows by itself, because RankingPanel passes the plan's monthlyPayment to projectPayoff (ranking.ts:164). If they answer yes but a loan has no bill, ask for it ('Add your monthly bill to each loan to see the term you have left') instead of guessing.

3. Date the balances (S). Add balanceAsOf?: string to Loan (types/index.ts:27) and set it in saveLoan (page.tsx:218) only when the loan is new or its balance changed. Use the oldest stamp across loans: show 'Balances as of {date}' under 'You owe' (page.tsx:454), label the table's first row with that date instead of 'Today' (PayoffRunway.tsx:336), and when it is more than 45 days old show a Notice on Loans, Payoff and Records (beside 'Save today's snapshot', HistoryPanel.tsx:67): 'These balances are from {date}. Update them from your servicer before relying on the dates here.' Keep projections counting from this month; do not silently re-anchor them.

## Severity

Every borrower past their first bill who looks at Standard, Graduated or Extended, the app's 'least overall' pick in many cases, is shown a payment below their real bill and a debt-free date late by exactly the time already paid (7.5% low and a year late after one year; 33% low and four years late after four), and the least-overall pick changes for roughly 1 in 12 to 1 in 4 of them. Being in repayment is the normal state of this audience and the form asks for the bill, so this is the typical borrower in the main flow; Plans states no such assumption and Payoff's 'payments start this month' does not say the term restarts, so no level is dropped. The return-visit and in-school parts on their own would be P2.

Proposed P0; independent severity judges said P0, P0; final P0.

## Verification

Confirmed by 8 of 8 skeptics across 3 filings (standards, reproduce and impact on the term-restart and return-visit filings; reproduce and standards on the in-school filing), with their own probes at both widths and the page clock moved by two different methods; the term-restart three kept P0, the return-visit three lowered P1 to P2, and the in-school two kept P2.
