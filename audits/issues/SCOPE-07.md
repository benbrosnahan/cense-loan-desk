# SCOPE-07: Payments already credited toward forgiveness ignored

**P1** · effort M · Borrowers the model does not fit yet

The app never asks how many payments already count toward forgiveness and prices IBR, RAP and PSLF from payment one, so a borrower with 120 months of IBR credit is told forgiveness comes in November 2046 after $48,120 when it comes in November 2036 after $24,060.

## Measured

No input exists for credit already earned. Profile (src/types/index.ts:110-120) holds only income, householdSize, employment, goal, extraPerMonth and selectedPlan; the questions card has 10 form controls (4 goal and 4 employer choices, income, household), none asks for payments already counted, and no page text mentions them. Neither ASSUMPTIONS (src/data/plans.ts:278) nor the README's 'Known modelling assumptions' lists this. calc.ts prices IBR at 240 payments (line 438) and RAP at 360 (line 457), always counted from today, and PlansPanel.tsx:253 prints the fixed forgivenessYears. 120-month case (new borrower, one Unsubsidized loan from 2014, $55,000 at 6.21%, income $48,000, household 1, private employer, goal 'I do not know yet', 120 months of IBR credit): the IBR card shows $201 a month ($200.50), 'In total, over 20 yr $48,120', 'Not picked because it costs $69,000 more overall', and under 'How this plan works' '$94,851 would still be left after 20 years' (November 2046); the RAP card shows 'In total, over 30 yr $57,600'; the hero shows 'Standard $616 a month ... This plan hands over the least money overall.' PSLF case ($80,000 at 5.84%, income $60,000, Government, 'Going for forgiveness', 100 PSLF payments already made on IBR): hero 'RAP $250 a month' and 'After 120 qualifying payments the rest is cancelled, tax free.'; RAP card 'In total, over 30 yr $90,000' and '$62,000 would still be left after 30 years'; IBR card 'In total, over 20 yr $72,120' and '$120,275 would still be left after 20 years'. 2010 borrower ($45,000 at 6.80%, income $50,000, 180 months credited): IBR $217.17, forgiveness November 2046 after $52,120. IBR cap: a borrower who began IBR owing $55,000 and now owes $62,000 at 6.21% on $110,000 income is shown $694.88, the 10-year amount on today's balance. Fuzz of 20,000 random new borrowers (goal 'Pay the least overall', private employer) with 12 to 140 months credited, correcting only the clock: the app's own pick changes in 2.1% to 2.5% of cases (3.7% to 5.1% at 120 to 143 months) across the filing run and every skeptic rerun, and in 0.8% (1.3%) across any goal and employer; ranked on total paid, as MONEY-01 proposes, it changes in 14.6% to 15.2% (26.5% to 27.7%).

## Required

Price only the payments still owed. 120-month case: 120 IBR payments left, forgiven November 2036 after $24,060 paid with $68,945 forgiven, so the IBR card should read 'costs $19,033 more overall' on the app's own metric; RAP has 240 left because IBR payments count toward it under (k)(8)(i)(C)(4), $38,400 paid. PSLF case: 685.219(c)(1)(iii) asks for 120 payments in total, so 20 remain, the last in July 2028: $5,000 paid on RAP ($6,010 on IBR) with about $79,000 ($81,861) cancelled tax free. A PSLF path priced 120 payments from today, which is what MONEY-02's fix alone would show, reads $30,000 on RAP ($36,060 on IBR) and November 2036. Outside PSLF the same borrower has 140 IBR payments left, because PSLF payments count toward IBR under (k)(4)(v) (July 2038, $42,070 paid, $97,765 forgiven), and 260 RAP payments left (July 2048, $65,000 paid). 2010 borrower: not a new borrower, so (k)(1) asks for 300 payments at 15%; with 180 credited, 120 remain at $325.75: November 2036, $39,090 paid, $32,888 forgiven. IBR cap: (f)(2)(ii) uses the balance when IBR began, so $616.43, not $694.88. The forgiven amounts above keep the app's monthly compounding of unpaid IBR interest; with the simple accrual MONEY-05 requires they are $81,777 instead of $81,861, $92,437 instead of $97,765 and $65,095 instead of $68,945, while totals paid and dates do not change.

## Reproduce

1. Open http://localhost:3100 in a fresh private window and click 'Add my first loan'. Type 09/01/2014 in 'Date it was paid out', choose 'Unsubsidized', type 55000 in 'What you owe on it today', replace whatever the form filled into 'Interest rate' with 6.21, then click 'Save loan'.
2. Open the Repayment plans tab. Leave 'I do not know yet', click 'Private sector', type 48000 in 'Yearly income, before tax' and keep 'Just me'.
3. Look for a place to say that 120 payments already count toward IBR forgiveness: the card asks only the four questions.
4. The IBR card shows 'In total, over 20 yr $48,120' and 'Not picked because it costs $69,000 more overall'; click 'How this plan works' to see '$94,851 would still be left after 20 years and gets forgiven'. The RAP card shows 'In total, over 30 yr $57,600'. With 120 months credited the rules give $24,060 paid, $68,945 forgiven, November 2036, and RAP $38,400.
5. PSLF: in a new private window add one Unsubsidized loan dated 09/01/2015 owing 80000, with 'Interest rate' set to 5.84, then on Repayment plans click 'Going for forgiveness' and 'Government' and type 60000 as income. The hero reads 'RAP $250 a month ... After 120 qualifying payments the rest is cancelled, tax free.' and the RAP card 'In total, over 30 yr $90,000', with no way to say 100 payments are already made (20 remain, July 2028, about $5,000).

Or run its test from `audits/`: `AUDIT_SHOW_KNOWN=1 npx playwright test -g "SCOPE-07"`.

## Fix

Ask for the counts once, then price only the payments still owed. Reuse MONEY-13's new-borrower test and land the two together.
1. src/types/index.ts: add idrCount, pslfCount and ibrStartBalance (numbers, default 0) to Profile (line 110) and DEFAULT_PROFILE (line 122), and paymentsLeft?: number to PlanEstimate (line 65). storage.ts:47 already spreads DEFAULT_PROFILE under saved data, so old profiles load with zeros.
2. src/app/page.tsx: in the questions card (lines 670-778), after the household field, add one optional Disclosure, 'Already made payments toward forgiveness?', with three numeric fields built like the income field: 'Payments counted toward income-driven forgiveness', 'Qualifying PSLF payments' and 'What you owed when you started IBR'. Hint: 'studentaid.gov shows your IDR and PSLF counts. If your loans show different counts, enter the smallest.' Say that RAP uses the IDR count as an approximation.
3. src/lib/calc.ts:

## Severity

Borrowers who already hold IDR or PSLF credit (months on IBR or another IDR plan, 10-year Standard payments, PSLF-qualifying payments) see wrong totals, forgiveness dates, forgiven amounts and 'costs $X more overall' lines on the IBR and RAP cards every time they open Repayment plans, whenever those plans end in forgiveness, about double the real remaining total at 120 months; nothing on screen says the count is assumed to be zero, so no level is dropped. Borrowers with no credit see correct figures and the top pick changed in only 0.8% to 2.5% of fuzzed cases, so this is P0-type harm limited to a recognizable group: P1.
